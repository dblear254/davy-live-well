Everyday health essentials

Start with one small, realistic habit.

🥗

Eat a varied diet

Include vegetables, fruit, beans or other protein, and whole grains when available. Limit excess salt, sugary drinks and highly processed foods.

🚶

Move regularly

Walking, dancing, cycling or active household work all count. Begin at a level that feels safe and build up gradually.

😴

Protect sleep and wellbeing

Keep a regular sleep routine where possible. Make time for rest, supportive relationships and stress-management habits.

Disease prevention

Prevention varies by person and local health advice.

🩺

Check-ups and screening

Ask a qualified health worker which checks are appropriate for your age, health history and circumstances. High blood pressure may have no obvious symptoms.

💉

Vaccination

Keep up with recommended vaccines for you and your family. Ask a health professional or local clinic about the current schedule.

🚭

Reduce avoidable risks

Avoid tobacco, avoid harmful alcohol use, use road safety measures, and follow prescribed treatment rather than stopping medicines on your own.

Hygiene and everyday safety

Simple actions can lower some health risks.

🧼

Clean hands

Wash with soap and safe running water, especially before preparing or eating food, after using the toilet, and after cleaning a child.

🥤

Safe water and food

Use safe drinking water. Keep raw and cooked foods separate, cook food thoroughly and store it safely.

🦟

Prevent mosquito bites

Where malaria occurs, follow local guidance on insecticide-treated nets, repellents and removing standing water. Seek care promptly for fever.

When to seek urgent help

Do not wait for an app

Get emergency medical help for signs such as severe difficulty breathing, chest pain, sudden weakness or trouble speaking, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, or a seizure that is new or prolonged. If you are in Kenya, use the locally available emergency service or go to the nearest emergency department.
